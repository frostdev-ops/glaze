# Features

Everything Glaze does, site by site. What changed in each version is in the [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md); what is kept and sent where is in the [privacy summary](privacy.md).

Glaze is a Chrome, Firefox and Safari extension that restyles Blackboard Ultra, Canvas and Moodle. It replaces the palette with one you
control, reshapes the pages around it, and adds small helpers you can see at a glance. It works on any
institution's Blackboard Ultra install, because every Ultra deployment lives under `/ultra/` on its own
domain, and on Canvas and Moodle, which Glaze recognizes on any school's domain.

Ultra's in-page route changes remain visible while Blackboard builds them. Only the initial document paint can wait
for Glaze's local stylesheets, with the existing one-second limit.
If Glaze is reloaded while an LMS tab stays open, its old extension API work stops until that tab reloads too.
Its animated scene also stops and releases its rendering resources.

The settings editor has ten categories: **Look, Navigation, Courses, Study, Course pages, Sharing, Mods, AI, Privacy and data, and About**. **All settings** opens More options and reveals advanced sections; search covers every control. Search works across tabs; section links such as `#blackboard/nav` and older editor links reveal the right tab. Section resets change their own preferences; resetting preferences keeps your palettes, pictures, local notes/tasks, bookmarks and share keys. The live preview uses fictional local pages at real desktop or phone CSS widths, with site/page/appearance controls. Focusing a Blackboard course's editor controls previews that course's appearance; Blackboard nicknames can be edited here as well as on its course cards. Sharing includes the Relay origin under All settings and an explicit connection test; saving stays offline.

## Mods

From Glaze 3.0.4: add backgrounds, animations, widgets, nav layouts and page tweaks made by others, each showing what it reads and why before you add it, or build your own widget with no code. Reviewed mods are in the [Mods gallery](https://frostdev.io/glaze/mods). Looks can have video and animated backgrounds too.

**User-installed mods:** Chrome can run JavaScript from mod bundles you choose to install, including author-signed channel updates. Glaze does not fetch mod code to run automatically from us. Code runs in an isolated sandbox without extension APIs; Firefox and Safari use declarative parts only. Courses, to-dos and grades are available only with declared and granted permissions, and only to a DOM-less Worker. Brokered network requests require a declared and granted exact HTTPS host and omit cookies and referrers. A mod with both data and network access can send that data to its granted hosts. A mod without data access may use a frame UI that can contact other sites; linked Look media is fetched by Glaze's worker from its chosen host. Mod bundles, grants, settings and per-mod storage stay on this device. **Remove** takes the mod's stored data, private-data mark, navigation switch and stop note with it unless you untick **Remove its stored data too**; an update that turns a no-code widget into a code mod asks first, and says it runs its author's code. See [PRIVACY.md](../PRIVACY.md) for channels and the optional signed blocklist.

## Surfaces

**Settings → Look → Shape and surfaces** controls content-panel opacity, blur, palette/custom color, accent/course tint, shadow strength/color, frost, depth and borders. Panels, native content rows, navigation, menus, tooltips and Glaze’s own apps share those controls across Blackboard Ultra, Canvas and Moodle. Surface backgrounds follow the Look’s corner radius. Automatic defaults retain each Look’s existing fills; custom transparency needs blur and a non-solid background. Small rows get no additional backdrop blur.

## Pets

All ten bundled companions, including Classic buddy, Cat and Bunny, have new climbing, sleeping, playing, peeking, XP and level-up reaction animations. Imported v1/v2 characters retain compatible fallback poses.

Choose one pet character for the habitat, Study buddy, Blackboard navigation badge and course-card pet. The original Cat and Bunny join the character selector, and Blackboard file previews refresh expired viewer tickets and retry while uploaded files convert.

**Animated pets:** the optional habitat keeps its existing XP and decorations. Choose Ember the fox, Luna the owl, Mochi the cat, Sprout the dragon, Pebble the capybara, Rime the arctic fox or the animated Glaze droplet, or import a local ChatGPT pet under **Settings → Study → Pet habitat**. Choose its `pet.json` and PNG/WebP sprite sheet together, a ZIP containing them, or the sprite sheet alone. Existing v1 (`1536 × 1872`) and v2 (`1536 × 2288`) sheets are supported; v2 adds sixteen gaze directions. Click a pet to wave, or focus it and press Enter/Space to jump. Animation pauses offscreen and in hidden pages, and reduced motion or Glaze Motion off shows a still frame. No account connection, pet service or upload is needed.

Your character choice is shared across this device: the habitat, Study buddy widget, navigation badge and course-card pet all use it. Each display keeps its own visibility setting. The original Cat and Bunny are also available in the habitat's character selector; their original artwork has idle and action poses. All displays wake during a focus timer, celebrate a completed focus block or a task checked in Better To Do, and respond to petting where interactive. These reactions add no XP. Progress, outfits and habitat decorations stay with each school's signed-in account. Removing a selected imported pet switches back to Classic buddy without removing progress or decorations. The navigation level uses the signed-in account directly and does not depend on planner-cache freshness.

## Share sessions

**Optional share sessions:** everything lives in **Settings → Sharing → Share sessions** (Ctrl/⌘K and To Do open it too). In Notes, **Share** on a note shares it in one step and copies an edit invite; **Shared notes** lists the account's shared notes with their invites and joins a pasted invite straight into Notes. Nothing connects until you create, join or resume. Share selected notes/a folder (including encrypted pictures and attachments), your current Look, or selected custom tasks with their steps and plans. Paste invites into Glaze rather than opening them in a web page. Anyone holding an edit invite can edit; a view invite can only read. Joining keeps a separate local copy; explicitly copy into a chosen local account to add new items. An editable copy follows updates; a viewer's copy is detached. A Look changes your theme only after **Use this Look** opens the existing editor preview and you apply it.

While a shared surface is open, changes sync through `https://share.frostdev.io` by default, end-to-end encrypted, with catch-up on reopening. **Pause sharing** stops connections; Glaze and your local copies still work without the relay. **Leave this device** forgets its keys; only the creator can delete the session from the relay. App copies remain. Only the creator can delete a session on demand, but on the default relay a share nobody has used for two years is deleted (one year if its creator opted into deletion after a year unused), and a share that was created but never written to is removed after two unused days; any member opening or changing a share counts as use. The device list shows self-chosen editing-device names and last-seen times, not verified identities; view-only devices cannot announce themselves. Pause, Leave and Delete remain available while a sync is busy; old queued updates cannot reconnect afterward.

**Settings → Sharing → Relay** (with **All settings** on) can select a custom HTTPS origin, or HTTP `localhost`/`127.0.0.1` for local testing. Saving only changes the synced preference for **new** sessions; existing sessions and backup keys keep their own server. **Test connection** explicitly checks `/health` with a five-second timeout and never enables sharing. Joining a custom-server invite first names the host and asks for confirmation in Glaze, before permission checks or connections. Restoring custom keys stays offline until Resume confirms any new hosts. Custom operators receive encrypted data and metadata under their own policies; Glaze cannot vouch for them. The default relay also limits how much new data one client (an address, or an IPv6 network) can store in a day (1 GiB), and everyone behind a shared school or campus address shares those limits, so a share may show as offline for a while when many students sync at once. When you remove or replace a note's file in a share, editors' devices delete the old file from the relay afterwards; a note too long to keep whole is refused with a warning rather than shared cut short. See [Run your own relay](../server/README.md) for Node/Docker, TLS proxies, disk/R2 storage, limits, backups and updates.

Notes **Back up or restore** opens **Settings → Privacy and data → Backup and reset**. **Back up…** saves one ZIP of settings and Looks, tasks and pet, every Notes account, dashboard notes, class schedules, GPA past terms, widgets and share keys; **Restore…** lists what a file holds, each ticked, and also takes older settings files and Notes ZIPs. Backups always include all share keys and creator delete tokens, even while sharing is paused: anyone with the backup can open those sessions and may change or delete them. Keep backups private. Restoring keys does not connect; resume explicitly. After a relay restore, **Re-upload retained files** sends this device's retained bytes encrypted again and publishes fresh references. Missing local bytes cannot be recreated. Caps are 20 sessions, 200 selected notes/tasks, 16 MiB session state, 64 MiB files per session and 200 MiB retained session files per device. Additional carriers need an optional adapter; arbitrary storage and LMS assignments are not shared.

## Everything else

- **Sidebar (Canvas and Blackboard Ultra, on by default)** — one column at the window's right edge: **This page** (the page's own sidebar, as the LMS made it: Recent Feedback and course actions, submission details, grade totals and What-If, mini calendars, quiz question links and timer; on Ultra the Courses page's column, a course outline's Faculty, Details & Actions and schedule, with Course notes and the syllabus, a discussion's details and participants, and recognized assessment Grades / Attempts, attempt Details & Information, or a submitted document's Rubric, integrated visually without moving native nodes; unrecognized attempt panels stay native) and under it **My widgets**, the widgets you dock there. On a fresh install, Better To Do and a compact **Glaze settings** widget start there for the first signed-in account, in that order; settings opens the existing editor and also starts in Glaze’s navigation bar. It can be moved, removed or re-added like any dock widget. Better To Do follows the page's course inside a course and all courses elsewhere, or **All courses** until you pick **Follow page** again; it takes the place of the page's own To Do. On Ultra's Courses page the sidebar replaces Glaze's widget column, and the widgets that were in it move in once, in their order. Widget menus show their supported destinations: the sidebar, the Dashboard (or Ultra's board), floating, or removal, keeping their data and old spots; **+ Add widgets** brings one back. In the sidebar, drag a widget by its handle or use the arrow keys to reorder it, drag the edge under it or use the arrow keys for its height, and drag the column's inner edge or use the arrow keys for its width (288–480 pixels); each change is announced. On a wide window the column keeps its room beside the page (collapse it to a strip), on a narrower one a **Sidebar** button opens it as a drawer; a page with nothing for it keeps no room, just **+ Add widgets** at the edge. Quizzes keep their normal layout. It stays above Glaze's footer and the bottom bar, keeps floating widgets clear of it, follows your Look, and works with the keyboard, reduced motion, high contrast, zoom and right-to-left languages. The dock layout is kept for each site and account on this device; removals of its initial To Do and settings widgets sync for that account. **Reset sidebar for this account** in its ⋯ menu starts the column over without moving your widgets. Settings → Navigation → **Sidebar** customizes it for every account: **Floating** (a glass card off the edges, the default, like the navigation's) or **Embedded** (set into the edge like the site's own column), or like Glaze's navigation; how a page opens it (as you left it, expanded, collapsed or only as a drawer); its name, header and Jump to widgets; its width until you drag it; This page or My widgets first, This page shown, folded or hidden; glass, solid or clear with blur and opacity; corners and spacing (the Look's by default); how much room the page keeps before it becomes a drawer (720 pixels, 900 for grades); which sites and kinds of page it shows on; To Do's starting scope; and **Absorb page panels into the sidebar**, which places recognized assessment information under This page while keeping native controls in place. On current Blackboard assessment pages, Grades / Attempts and Details & Information scroll with the widgets and stay available in the drawer. Unrecognized layouts keep their original placement; older integrated panels return to the page when the sidebar cannot display them safely. Focus mode and print restore native layouts, and a document viewer never loses width to the sidebar. A Look you share carries its style, surface and corners, never your layout. Turning it off gives the page its own sidebar, the Dashboard's To Do and Ultra's column board back exactly as they were; your dock is kept for when you turn it on again.
- **Course cards as widgets on Canvas** — on a fresh Dashboard, the cards of Canvas's card view join its starter widgets, in Canvas's order (a board you empty yourself stays empty); **Edit layout → + Course cards** adds or removes courses. They are drawn as Canvas's own cards (nickname, code, term, color, picture and its row of links with their unread counts) with Glaze's grades, new-item badges, tasks, bookmarks and card styles; they move, snap, resize, fold and remove like other tiles, up to 48 per school. Canvas's own card view steps aside while they are there; **Show Canvas’s course cards** brings it back (for moving or hiding cards in Canvas), and **Show course card widgets** returns. They read only what Canvas's Dashboard already asked for.
- **Course cards as widgets on Ultra** — a fresh Dashboard fills once with your courses; a board you empty stays empty. Add cards with **Edit layout → + Course cards** on Courses, or pick **+ Course card** on the Dashboard. Each card moves, snaps, resizes, folds and removes like other tiles; each board keeps its own layout, up to 48 course cards per school. The cards start in the native list’s area, in course/term order. **Show Ultra’s course list** restores its search, filters and term picker; **Show course cards** returns to your tiles. Removing every course card or hiding the board restores the native list. Cards reuse loaded metadata, grades, progress, new-work badges, bookmarks and stored tasks; unavailable data stays absent until the existing readers answer. No extra course, gradebook or planner request is made.
- **Blackboard Ultra appearance** — the Courses page shares Canvas’s card editor and appearance settings: colors, gradients, pictures, crop, filters and overlay; grid sizing, condensed cards, hover effects, particles and pets. Open a course card's Glaze menu, then **Card style and bookmarks**. In Ultra list view, banner styles and corners apply; row dimensions and labels stay native. The global navigation shares Canvas's background, label and size controls, with contrast checks in the editor. On recognized Ultra submission reviews, the native Rubric appears in This page using its existing column's room; the document viewer stays at least as wide, with its iframe kept in place. Unknown structures keep their native layout.
- **Blackboard global widgets** — a widget you choose to float stays available across Blackboard pages, including the native Institution tab, with one copy. It keeps clear of either navigation rail, course tab strips and the footer; a small window gets a scrolling stack. The Dashboard's own layout stays separate.

- **Palettes** — six presets, 14 colour tokens, your own saved palettes, and automatic light/dark palettes that follow the system or a schedule (dark from 20:00 to 08:00, say). Keep quizzes light (off by default) leaves quizzes you're taking and external tools such as New Quizzes unthemed on Canvas.
- **Look** — fonts for text, headings and code (two bundled; about forty more from Google Fonts and Nerd Fonts, or any Google Font by name, each previewed in its own face), text size, line height, corner radius, density, depth and glass.
- **Backgrounds** — solid; a gradient you shape (linear, radial, conic or mesh; angle, blend, spread); a drifting aurora; your own image; a looping video, animated picture (WebP or AVIF) or Lottie animation from a link or a file (a link is fetched and checked by Glaze's worker, never loaded by the page: 12 MiB for a video, 6 MiB for a picture, 4096 pixels a side; always muted, held on its first frame when motion is off, paused while the tab is hidden); or eleven slow animated scenes (three.js, drawn at low resolution; two for Halloween, a moonlit graveyard and a pumpkin patch; Frostdev's living paper-cut topography) with one Intensity slider. Palette colors or your own.

  addresses so the browser can recover from a cached failure. Changing the background or turning
  Glaze off and on permits another attempt; refused shaders and missing media keep their fallback.
- **Motion** — Off, Subtle or Expressive. Reduced-motion users get Off automatically.
- **Course colors** — every course its own color, from Ultra's stripes blended into your palette or set one by one, on course pages, Messages, Grades and the calendar.
- **Tooltips and hints** — themed tooltips. Hover a date for how far off it is, a grade for its share.
- **Extras** — score bars, due-date status ("in 2 days · Unopened", "Submitted", "Submitted late", "Graded · 18/20") and grade percentages, folder previews, course progress, new-grade and new-content badges, a what-if grade calculator that keeps your scores, a Ctrl+K command palette for courses, items, pages, settings and what's due this week (also in the popup) that also searches your courses on Canvas and Ultra, remembering your position in Canvas Modules, a backup of what you type in Blackboard's editors, Download all, which saves a folder's files as one zip, and focus mode for course documents (on by default); due-date urgency, shorter descriptions, reading width, keyboard shortcuts, dark documents (dimmed or inverted) and hiding page parts you don't use (off by default).
- **Blackboard submission status** — Blackboard saved drafts stay unsubmitted in due-date badges and Better To Do; attempt dates or multiple attempts alone do not prove a submission. Completed external-tool items keep Blackboard's native completion when it reports no submission status.
- **On Canvas** — the same extras, each where Canvas keeps the same thing: progress, previews, new-item badges, your place and Download all on Modules; score bars and percentages on Grades and Assignments; due-date status and urgency on Assignments, Modules and To Do; tint, a Look per course and card grades on the Dashboard (click one for the course's grades, or keep them hidden until you point at the card); draft backup for new Inbox messages to individual users in uniquely named courses. A **what-if calculator** above a course's grades works out your grade by Canvas's own rules (weights, dropped scores, excused work, grading periods) and matches Canvas's totals: change real scores, leave work out, add pretend assignments and see what you'd need for a target. It keeps what you change on your device until the real grade arrives, and never writes to Canvas. An optional **GPA** on the Dashboard gives your term and cumulative GPA on a 4.0 or plus/minus scale, weighted for honors and AP if you like, with credits, a grading scale and a what-if grade per course. Add your **past terms** by hand or from a transcript: its text, a PDF, a photo or screenshot, a web page or a Word file (read on your device and shown for review first; pass/fail, withdrawn and transfer courses are listed but not counted) for an overall GPA, a **trend** chart with the same figures as a table, and a **goal**: what this term needs, and one way there from your grades now, with the lowest percent for each letter and what each course's work left needs. Kept on your device; Ctrl+K opens it. Where Canvas has its own (editor recovery, folder zips, unread counts), Glaze leaves it to Canvas, themed; a course card has one menu, in the place of Canvas’s ⋮, for its nickname and color (saved in Canvas, as Canvas’s own menu saves them), tint, Look and card style, and Canvas’s own menu is one press from it for moving or hiding the card; Canvas's own what-if scores still work beside Glaze's. Course progress counts module completion requirements, so a course without any shows no bar.
- **Grades on Blackboard Ultra** — the same **what-if calculator**, under a course's overall grade: by the course's own overall-grade recipe where Ultra gives it to students (checked first against Blackboard's own figure), otherwise an estimate from your share of the points in graded work, shown beside Blackboard's figure and marked as one. A failed load retries after 1, 4 and 15 seconds, pauses while the page or course tab is hidden, and then offers **Try again**. A partial gradebook waits for a complete answer without losing your edits. Ultra's system categories use your chosen language; teacher-created names stay as written. Scores typed in Glaze's earlier Ultra calculator move over. The **GPA**, with past terms, trends and a goal, sits on Ultra's Grades page for this term's courses; where Blackboard doesn't show a course's letter cut-offs, Glaze uses your scale for it (typed, or from its syllabus) or a common one. **Syllabus import** is on a course's outline and reads a document in the course, a file or pasted text; **Insights** opens from the Courses page or Ctrl+K.
- **Insights (Canvas and Blackboard, off by default)** — your term in numbers, from a button on the Dashboard or Ctrl+K: the share of work you handed in on time per course, each course's grade over the term as a sparkline, the work due each week with the busiest weeks ahead, how often your Better To Do plans were kept, and a summary card you can save as a picture. Every chart has a table beside it. Worked out on your device from your own Canvas; only a small summary of this term is kept there, until you clear it.
- **Better To Do (Canvas and Blackboard, on by default)** — Glaze's own To Do list in place of Canvas's on the Dashboard and a course's home page, and on Blackboard in place of the To Do on the Courses page and beside a course's content (that course only): grouped by day in your Canvas time zone (on Blackboard, your browser's) (past due, today, tomorrow, this week, later), with what's done, what's graded and announcements apart; a free custom timeframe; your own tasks, steps, a day to do something on and timed focus sessions, kept on your device; and, for teachers, work to grade with grading progress. Ticking can also mark the item done in Canvas's own planner, if you switch that on; on Blackboard a tick always stays on your device. Courses Canvas no longer lists as active (on Blackboard: hidden or closed courses) are left out; if the course list can't be loaded, every course shows rather than any being hidden. On Blackboard, work comes from its To Do list and calendar, with what you handed in, late, missing or excused from each course's gradebook; when Blackboard can't send its calendar whole, the list says some work may be missing.
- **Announcements in a pop-up (Canvas, Blackboard and Moodle, on by default)** — an announcement opens in Glaze's pop-up over the page instead of leaving it, from Better To Do, widgets, Ctrl+K search and each site's own lists: on Blackboard a course's Announcements and the Activity Stream; on Canvas Announcements, Recent Activity and the Dashboard; on Moodle the news forum and the Latest announcements block. The pop-up holds the title, any available course, author and date details, the post itself (made safe: no scripts, frames or styles, pictures only from your school's site, links in a new tab), its files, and **Open in Canvas**, **Open in Blackboard** or **Open in Moodle** for the site's own page. Opening an unread announcement marks it read on Canvas, as Canvas's page does, and on Moodle Moodle's own request marks its posts read where the forum tracks them automatically; Blackboard isn't marked, and a preview never marks anything. Ctrl- or ⌘-click, Shift-click or a middle click still opens the page in a new tab, and if the announcement can't be read the site's page opens instead. It's a real dialog: focus moves in, Escape or Close closes it and focus goes back to the link; it fits a phone-width window, larger text and right-to-left languages, and wears your Look. With **Pop-up animation → Genie**, it grows out of an on-screen link with Expressive motion and scales in with Subtle; **Fade** fades it in. Motion off or reduced, or **None**, makes it appear at once. Point at or focus an announcement in Better To Do or a site’s own list for a moment to preview its first lines (on Moodle, what the list shows); Escape hides the preview. **Settings → Course pages → Announcements** has **Open announcements** (in a pop-up or on the site's page) and **Preview on hover**; with **All settings**, **Pop-ups** sets each entry point (Better To Do, widgets and search; Blackboard; Canvas; Moodle) to Default, Pop-up or Site's page, its preview on or off, the preview's delay and lines, and **Pop-up animation** (Genie, Fade or None). On a Blackboard course's Announcements, **Mark all as read** ticks each unread announcement's own read circle in turn (scrolling, a click or a key stops it), and ticking one keeps your place in the list.
- **Rubrics (Canvas and Blackboard Ultra, on by default)** — an assignment's rubric, with your published scores and feedback, read-only. On Ultra's assessment overview it replaces the rubric entry in Assessment Details when its columns fit, otherwise its rubric link opens it in the same pop-up; on Canvas the folded rubric's toggle opens the pop-up, and a rubric Canvas already shows stays as it is. The site's own view stays whenever Glaze can't read or recognize the rubric. **Settings → Course pages → Documents and quizzes → Rubrics**: Automatic, Pop-up or The site's own; with **All settings**, **Pop-ups → Show inline when there's room**.
- **Export calendar (.ics) (Canvas and Blackboard)** — from Better To Do's Export button or Ctrl+K: one file of your Canvas or Blackboard deadlines, your own tasks with a due date, your "do on" days and focus sessions (as events at their times) and, if you show it, your Gradescope work, for the next 2, 4, 8 or 16 weeks or the rest of the term. Import it into Google Calendar, Outlook or Apple Calendar yourself; each item keeps the same identifier, so importing a newer file updates it where the calendar supports that. Made on your device and saved by your browser; the file holds your course and assignment names.
- **Google Calendar sync (Chrome, off until you connect)** — keeps a calendar named Glaze in your Google account up to date with the same events and choices as the export, from Glaze's settings. Chrome signs you in with Google; Glaze asks only to manage calendars it makes (`calendar.app.created`), so it can't see or touch your other calendars. Deadlines, tasks and classes mirror Canvas and your schedule: edit one in Google and Glaze writes it back, delete one and it stays deleted. Move or delete a "do on" day or focus session in Google and the plan follows in Better To Do. No reminders on the events. **Link another device** shows a code to paste on your other computer, so both keep the one calendar; **Disconnect** revokes Glaze's access and can delete the calendar. It syncs from Canvas and Blackboard pages (on open, every 15 minutes, and soon after your plans change); there's no Glaze server, and requests go only to Google. - **Tasks on course cards (Canvas and Blackboard, off by default)** — each Dashboard card (on Blackboard, each card on the Courses page) lists its course's next items (4 by default, up to 10, then how many more), soonest first, with the date and a status chip (submitted, graded, late, missing, excused); done items can stay crossed out. It follows Better To Do's timeframe and done rules and reuses its copy of your planner, on or off. The card grows to fit its list.
- **Search in your courses (Canvas and Blackboard Ultra, on by default)** — from two characters in Ctrl/⌘K, find titles in your courses, this course first, then this term's, up to 24 results. Ultra searches this course and open courses in this term, including released public text of documents, announcements and discussion topics, without opening posts or marking anything read. Unknown or unsupported release conditions are omitted. Content results use Ultra's own redirect; announcements open in the pop-up or on the site's page, as chosen in settings; discussions open their course tool list. Answers are reused for five minutes; bounded stale entries are evicted on later cache use and all search data is cleared when search or Glaze is switched off. Typing again or closing cancels pending requests. Ultra asks at most three lists at a time, 30 search requests per query and two pages (up to 400 items) per list, across at most 100 selected courses, so very large courses or course lists may be incomplete. No search text or answers are stored.
- **Canvas's document viewer** — PDFs and files in Canvas's viewer wear your palette (background, toolbar, thumbnails) with the pages left readable, or dimmed or inverted in a dark palette. **Pen, highlighter and eraser** (off by default): any color and size, undo and redo, clear page, export and import as a file, a page saved as a picture; kept on your device per site, account and file, never sent anywhere, and beside Canvas's own annotations, never over them.
- **Syllabus import (Canvas and Blackboard, off by default)** — read a course's syllabus on your device: Canvas's own Syllabus page, a PDF, a photo or screenshot, a Word (.docx), PowerPoint (.pptx), web page or text file, or pasted text, in English, German, Spanish, French, Dutch or Portuguese. Glaze finds the professor (name, e-mail, office, office hours), class times, exam and due dates (in the course's term year), the grading scale and the weights, then shows every field for you to correct, each with the line it came from and "Check this" on a guess. Then the professor, class times and coming dates show on the course home; the scale can become the course's in your GPA, the dates your own Better To Do tasks, and the weights show beside the what-if calculator (Canvas's own weights still count). On Blackboard, Read this document lists each of the course's documents under its own title and reads the files it links (PDFs, Word, PowerPoint, pictures). Deadlines given by course week ("7th day of week 3", "Week 5 – Friday") become dates, with a **Week 1 starts** date in the review that moves them all; weekly rules ("quizzes close every Sunday") become one date each week; exam windows and "week of" items land on their last day; several items on one line become one task each; points-only grading becomes percents; TA and tutor office hours come with their role. Pictures keep a table's row and a chart's label with its value together. What Glaze can't read for sure stays empty. Edit or delete it from the course home; Ctrl+K has Import a syllabus on any course page.
- **AI (off by default)** — Settings → AI connects a model on this device (Chrome's built-in model where the computer supports it; Apple Intelligence in Safari on macOS and iOS 27) or an OpenAI-compatible endpoint you choose (OpenAI, OpenRouter, Ollama, LM Studio and others; key optional), and lists which features may use it. ## Glaze navigation

up in Settings → **Navigation**. **Placement**: a side rail, open with labels or icons only (with a tooltip on each, which Escape dismisses, and
